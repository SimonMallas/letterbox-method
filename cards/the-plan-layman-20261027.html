What already works

The filing cabinet

letters

Every message between agents — and from you, from your phone — is a letter: addressed, dated, kept forever. Corrections are new letters that name what they replace. Nothing is ever edited or deleted.

The exact-answer machine

QEM

Ask the envelopes, not a model: what's owed to me? which version stands? did that ever happen? Counted, exact, and it says where it looked — an empty answer is never dressed up as "no".

The notebook habit

the map

Agents keep quick-reference notes that cite the letter behind every fact and carry a bookmark of how fresh they are. Fast to read; safe to throw away and rebuild.

What we're proving

the research — three honest questions

Does the notebook stay true?

Q1 · honesty over months

We measure how far notebooks drift from the cabinet, and how fast they get corrected. Nobody publishes this measurement — we'll be the first with a number, good or bad.

open question

Can fuzzy memory be trusted?

Q2 · the optional helper

If we add a "sounds like…" helper, it must show the letter it means — or say nothing. Never a plausible sentence with no source. Parked until the rule can be enforced, not just requested.

open question

What if there are two offices?

Q3 · records across machines

Two machines, two cabinets — kept in step because letters only ever get added, never edited. The hard parts (undoing a letter, proving delivery) get researched before anything is built.

The ground rules — printed on the box

  • The record is never rewritten. A correction is a new entry that names what it replaces.
  • Everything above the record is disposable. Any notebook or index can be deleted and rebuilt — nothing is lost.
  • Nothing is claimed that wasn't searched. Every "nobody does this" comes with the list of where we looked.
  • The person is a seat, not a supervisor. You write letters like anyone else — from your phone, your menu bar, or Slack.
